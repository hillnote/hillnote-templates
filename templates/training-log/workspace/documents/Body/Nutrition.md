# Nutrition

Eating to train, not to diet. The aim is enough protein to recover, enough carbohydrate to run well, and no drama. Not medical or dietary advice: if you have a health condition, or want a plan built for you, see a registered dietitian.

## Protein

Most research on people who lift and run puts a useful daily target somewhere between 1.6 and 2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight. At ${bodyweight_kg} kg, 1.8 g/kg works out at:

**${=bodyweight_kg * 1.8} g of protein a day**, or about ${=round(bodyweight_kg * 1.8 / 4)} g at each of four meals.

Spreading it across the day helps more than one huge serving. A palm-sized portion of meat, fish or tofu, a big pot of yoghurt, or three eggs is roughly 25 to 30 g.

## Carbohydrate

Carbohydrate is the fuel for hard sessions. Match it to the day:

| Day | Carbohydrate | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Rest or mobility | Lower | Normal meals, plenty of vegetables |
| Easy run or strength | Moderate | A fist of rice, pasta or potatoes at each main meal |
| Intervals or long run | Higher | Add a carbohydrate snack before and after |

## Fuelling runs

- **Under an hour:** nothing needed beyond normal meals. Water if it's warm.
- **Over about 75 minutes:** 30 to 60 g of carbohydrate an hour, starting after 30 to 45 minutes. One gel or a banana is roughly 25 g.
- **Race morning:** a familiar breakfast two to three hours before. Nothing new on race day.

## Around lifting

A meal with protein and carbohydrate within a couple of hours either side of the session is plenty. The timing matters far less than the daily total.

## A sample training day

| Meal | What | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | Porridge with milk, yoghurt and berries | 30 g |
| Lunch | Chicken, rice and vegetables | 35 g |
| After training | Glass of milk and a banana | 10 g |
| Dinner | Salmon, potatoes, greens | 35 g |
| Evening | Greek yoghurt with honey | 15 g |

That's about 125 g, right around the target.

## Race week

:::tabs
:::tab "Three days out"
Keep training light and eat normally, with a little more carbohydrate at each meal: an extra slice of toast, a bigger bowl of pasta. There is no need to stuff yourself for a 10k.
:::endtab
:::tab "Race morning"
A breakfast you have eaten before a hard session, two to three hours before the start: porridge with banana, or toast with honey. A few sips of water in the last hour, and a gel 15 minutes before the start only if you have practised with one.
:::endtab
:::tab "After the race"
Within an hour or two, a proper meal with protein and carbohydrate. Then take a few easy days before deciding on the next goal.
:::endtab
:::endtabs

## Water and sleep

Drink to thirst, and a bit more on hot days or long runs; pale urine is a fine guide. Sleep is the cheapest recovery tool there is: the Body metrics sheet tracks it beside resting heart rate, and the two usually move together.
