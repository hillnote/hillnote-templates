# Channel strategy

**${channel_name}** explains the science of everyday cooking in videos short enough to watch while the kettle boils. ${host_name} tests one kitchen question at a time, with a scale, a thermometer and an honest result, even when the result is boring.

:::toc "On this page"
:::

## Who it's for

Home cooks aged 25 to 44 who follow recipes but want to know *why* they work. They cook four or five nights a week, they've watched the big recipe channels, and they're tired of being told what to do without being told why.

**What they want**

- To fix the thing that went wrong last time
- A reason they can repeat at dinner
- Short videos with an honest answer

**What puts them off**

- Gadget hype and an affiliate link in every sentence
- Experiments with an answer decided in advance
- Long intros

## Pillars

Every video belongs to one pillar. If an idea doesn't fit any of them, it isn't one of our videos.

:::tabs
:::tab "Myth or fact"
Test a kitchen belief with a fair experiment and give a clear verdict.

**Example:** does salt make water boil faster?

**Format:** long video, with a Short cut from the verdict. About a third of what we make.
:::endtab
:::tab "Why it works"
Explain the science behind a technique people already use.

**Example:** why pasta water makes a sauce glossy.

**Format:** long video or Short. The backbone of the channel, about 40% of output.
:::endtab
:::tab "Try this at home"
A small experiment viewers can repeat tonight with what's in the cupboard.

**Example:** ice cream in a bag, with salt and ice.

**Format:** Shorts first. They travel furthest on TikTok and Instagram.
:::endtab
:::tab "Kitchen gear"
What a tool actually does, measured, without hype.

**Example:** what cast iron seasoning really is.

**Format:** long video, at most once a month. This is where sponsors fit most naturally, so keep the bar high.
:::endtab
:::endtabs

## Voice

- **Curious, not clever.** Ask the question the viewer is already asking.
- **Show the number.** If the scale says 212 grams, the viewer sees 212 grams.
- **Admit the boring result.** "It made no difference" is a result, and viewers trust you more for saying it.
- **No fake urgency.** Nothing is "insane", nobody "won't believe" anything.

## Hook formulas

The first fifteen seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest. Every hook uses one of these.

| Formula | Example | Works for |
|---|---|---|
| The myth | "Half the internet says salt makes water boil faster. I tested it." | Myth or fact |
| The wrong thing | "Your cookies didn't spread because of the recipe. It was the butter." | Why it works |
| The test | "I baked the same dough six ways. One change doubled the spread." | Any experiment |
| The number | "This pan is 40 degrees hotter in the middle than at the edge." | Kitchen gear |
| The visual | Open on the result, then rewind: "Every one of these is the same recipe." | Shorts |
| The question | "Why does bread go stale even in a sealed bag?" | Why it works |

**Rules:** say the hook in under 15 seconds, show something in the first 2, and never promise what the video doesn't deliver.

## Titles

- Put the curious bit first. Search results cut titles at around 70 characters and phones at around 50.
- Under 60 characters is the target; 100 is YouTube's hard limit.
- One word in capitals at most, and only if it's the point.
- The title and the thumbnail work together: the thumbnail asks, the title answers. Never repeat the same words in both.

Check a title against every platform's limit with the **Title check** view of the content calendar.

## Thumbnails

- Three words, maximum. If it needs a sentence, it's a title.
- One face and one object: ${host_name}'s face reacting, the food doing the thing.
- Before and after, split down the middle, whenever there's a test.
- Colours from the [[Brand and thumbnails|doc:documents/Brand/Brand%20and%20thumbnails.moodboard.md]] board: tomato red for the one word that matters, cast iron for the background.

## The repurposing ladder

One long video becomes:

1. **Two Shorts**: the verdict, and the single most surprising moment. Each under 45 seconds, with its own hook.
2. **One carousel**: the five facts from the video, one per slide, plus a cover and a closing slide.
3. **One newsletter section**: the story behind the test. What went wrong, what surprised us, what we'd test next.

Run `/repurpose` on a finished script to draft all three.
