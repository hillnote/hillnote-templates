# ICP and personas

Who ${product} is for, who it isn't for, and the three people involved in every deal. Every campaign brief names one persona from this page as its primary audience.

:::toc "On this page"
:::

## Ideal customer profile

| Trait | In | Out |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Agencies and consultancies that sell projects or retainers | Product companies with a self-serve funnel |
| Size | 10 to 50 people, 3 to 15 of whom deal with clients | Solo freelancers; firms over 200 |
| Sales motion | Referral and pitch-led, 5 to 40 open deals at a time | High-volume inbound with a dedicated sales team |
| Current tools | A spreadsheet, a shared inbox, maybe an abandoned CRM | A configured enterprise CRM with an admin |
| Trigger | A deal lost to slow follow-up | "The spreadsheet is fine" |

**The best fit, in one line:** a 25-person design or marketing agency whose founder still runs the pipeline from memory and a spreadsheet, and has just lost a deal because nobody followed up.

## Buying triggers

1. **The dropped ball.** A good lead went cold because the follow-up sat in someone's inbox.
2. **The second salesperson.** The founder hires someone to sell and realises the pipeline only exists in their head.
3. **The forecast question.** A partner, investor or bank asks what's coming in next quarter, and nobody can answer.
4. **The CRM that failed.** They bought a big CRM, nobody kept it up to date, and they went back to the spreadsheet.

## Personas

:::tabs
:::tab "Agency owner"
**Role:** founder or managing director. Signs the cheque and still sells the biggest deals.

**Goals**

- Know what revenue is coming in over the next 90 days
- Stop being the only person who remembers every lead

**Frustrations**

- Every CRM they've tried needed someone to feed it
- Losing deals they should have won

**In their words:** "I don't want a CRM. I want to stop losing deals I've already half won."

**Primary audience for:** the launch campaign, the pricing page and the webinar.
:::endtab
:::tab "Ops lead"
**Role:** operations or studio manager. Chooses the tool, sets it up and gets the team to use it.

**Goals**

- One place for every client conversation
- Fewer "who's following up on this?" messages

**Frustrations**

- Tools that take a month to set up
- Training people who never log in again

**In their words:** "If it needs a training session, it won't get used."

**Primary audience for:** onboarding emails, setup guides and the automation templates.
:::endtab
:::tab "Account manager"
**Role:** runs client relationships and renewals. Uses the CRM every day, but never chose it.

**Goals**

- Know what was promised to a client, and when
- Spot renewals and upsells early

**Frustrations**

- Copying notes between email, chat and the CRM
- Being blamed for follow-ups nobody assigned

**In their words:** "Just tell me who I need to email today."

**Primary audience for:** product tours, customer stories and in-app messages.
:::endtab
:::endtabs

## Where they spend time

| Channel | What works there | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Founder stories, short opinion posts, carousels | Launch ads and organic posts |
| Search | "CRM for agencies", "agency pipeline template" | Google Ads and the SEO cluster |
| Partner tools | Accounting and project tools they already pay for | Partner co-marketing |
| Email | A weekly newsletter they actually open | The launch email sequence |
| Peer communities | Agency owner groups and community forums | Webinar promotion |

## Voice of the customer

From twelve customer interviews this quarter, anonymised. Use these words in copy before inventing new ones.

> "I don't need more fields. I need fewer things to remember."

> "We tried a big CRM. It turned into a very expensive address book."

> "The follow-up is where we lose. Not the pitch."

> "If it did the boring bits on its own, I'd pay for it tomorrow."

Most of the quotes come back to one theme, tagged #follow-up-gap wherever it appears: the days between the pitch and the signature, when nobody owns the next step.
