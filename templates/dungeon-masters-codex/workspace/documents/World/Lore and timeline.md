# Lore and timeline

What an educated person on the Shattered Coast knows, or thinks they know. Safe to share with players; the truth behind it is in the [[Campaign bible|doc:documents/World/Campaign%20bible.md]].

## The Ruin

Before the Ruin, the coast was one long shore ruled from **Veyr**, a city of bronze towers whose sea-kings commanded the tides with enormous bells. The greatest was the Deep Bell, said to be as tall as a temple.

In a year of terrible storms, the last sea-king rang the Deep Bell to drive the sea back. The sea answered. In one night the cliffs fell, the shore broke into the islands and sea stacks now called **the Teeth**, and Veyr sank beneath them. The calendar still counts from that night: years **After the Ruin**, written AR.

## Timeline

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 0 AR | **The Ruin.** Veyr sinks; the coast breaks into cliffs, marsh and the Teeth |
| 1–300 AR | The Wreck Years. Ships break on the new rocks; the marsh folk of Saltreach become the Saltborn |

## Gods of the coast

:::tabs
:::tab "The Lantern"
**God of guidance, safe harbour and the honest flame.**

Symbol: a candle inside a ring. Every lighthouse is a shrine, and sailors touch a candle before casting off. Clerics of the Lantern train at the Weeping Abbey and swear never to let a light go out while a ship is at sea.

The Lantern asks for honesty and vigilance, and forgives almost anything confessed by candlelight.
:::endtab
:::tab "Old Brine"
**The hungry sea.**

Symbol: a hook, or nothing at all. Old Brine has no temples. People bargain with it rather than worship it: a coin over the side before a voyage, a drop of blood on the bow before a storm.

Old Brine asks for a share. The coast has never agreed on how big a share is enough.
:::endtab
:::tab "The Quiet Lady"
**Goddess of death and mercy, who gathers the drowned.**

Symbol: a closed eye. Her priests sing the dead below at every burial at sea, and a family that loses someone to the water leaves a bowl of salt on the doorstep for a month.

The Quiet Lady asks that the dead be let go. Her priests are deeply uneasy about the bells.
:::endtab
:::endtabs
