# Encounter building

A good fight is short, has a choice in it, and changes something in the story. These rules of thumb work with any d20 game and take a minute at the table.

## Count actions, not monsters

The side with more actions usually wins. One big monster against ${party_size} characters gets one turn for every ${party_size} of theirs, so it needs extra actions (the Choirmother's Chorus), minions, or terrain on its side. Four weak monsters are often more dangerous than one strong one.

## How long will it last?

Aim for three rounds. Fights shorter than that feel trivial, and longer ones drag.

Our party of ${party_size} level-${party_level} characters deals roughly ${=party_size * 6} damage a round between them, after misses. That's what the **Rounds to drop** column in the Monsters database uses: hit points divided by 24. Update the 24 in the formula as the party levels up.

- **Total monster hit points** for a three-round fight: about ${=party_size * 6 * 3}.
- **Total monster damage per round**: keep it under a quarter of the party's combined hit points, unless you mean it to be deadly.

## Give every fight a twist

Pick one:

- **Terrain:** rising tide, a slippery stair, a burning ship, fog that limits sight to 30 feet.
- **A goal other than killing:** rescue the ferryman, grab the ledger, ring (or silence) the bell before round 3.
- **A clock:** reinforcements arrive in round 4. The tide floods the room one step every round.
- **Morale:** most creatures flee at half hit points. Cultists surrender if their singer falls.

## Running away is a feature

Tell the players openly when a fight looks like a bad idea ("the golem shrugs off Brakka's trident like rain"). A retreat that leads to a clever plan is a better session than a total party kill.
