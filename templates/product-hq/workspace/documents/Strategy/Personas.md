# Personas

Three people decide whether ${product} sticks. Design for the first, delight the second, and don't surprise the third.

:::tabs
:::tab "Ops lead"
**Role:** Head of operations or regional ops manager. Buys the product and lives in it.

**Goals**
- Hit SLA targets without heroics
- Walk into the Monday meeting already knowing the answer

**Frustrations**
- Spends Monday mornings stitching exports together
- Finds out about problems from customers

**Jobs to be done**
- "When the week starts, help me see where we are slipping so I can move people before it costs us."

> "I don't need more charts. I need to know which three things to worry about today."
:::endtab
:::tab "Dispatcher"
**Role:** Assigns jobs and drivers all day. Uses the product in short bursts between calls.

**Goals**
- Keep today's jobs on time
- Spot a driver or route in trouble before it cascades

**Frustrations**
- Dashboards that take ten seconds to load
- Filters that reset every time they switch tabs

**Jobs to be done**
- "When a job is about to breach, tell me early enough that I can still reassign it."

> "If it's not on one screen, I won't see it."
:::endtab
:::tab "Finance partner"
**Role:** Approves the renewal and expansion. Opens the product once a month, if that.

**Goals**
- A clear cost per seat and evidence of use
- Exports that drop straight into their own reporting

**Frustrations**
- Paying for seats nobody uses
- Surprises on the invoice

**Jobs to be done**
- "When renewal comes up, show me that the team actually uses this."

> "Show me usage and I'll sign. Show me a slide deck and I won't."
:::endtab
:::endtabs

## Using personas in specs

Every PRD names its primary persona in the first paragraph. If a feature serves the finance partner at the expense of the dispatcher, say so explicitly and get it agreed in review.
