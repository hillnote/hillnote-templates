Commissioned for the winter issue, "Things we lost". The piece lives in [[Why we stopped fixing things|doc:documents/Pieces/Pieces/Why%20we%20stopped%20fixing%20things.md]]; the deadline is its publish date.

## The pitch as sent

**Subject:** Pitch for "Things we lost": why we stopped fixing things

Dear Odile,

My kettle died at fourteen months. The repair quote was more than a new one, and the man who gave it apologised. I'd like to write 3,000 words for "Things we lost" on why that happens: not because people stopped caring about repair, but because everything around repair got cheap. Products built to be replaced, spare parts priced to discourage, and skills nobody passed on.

The essay would move from my kettle to the people who still fix things anyway: a cobbler who has resoled one pair of boots for eleven years, and a monthly repair café where 29 of 41 broken things went home working.

I write **${newsletter_name}**, a fortnightly letter about repair read by about 2,100 people, and I'm working on a book of essays on the same subject. My most recent issue is "In praise of the visible mend".

Best,
${pen_name}

## What came back

Odile said yes in eight days and asked for the repair café scene to sit in the middle, not the end. That note became the structure.

## Lessons

- The first version of this pitch, to The Weekend Ledger, led with prices. It was [[declined|doc:documents/Pieces/Submissions/The%20economics%20of%20a%20cheap%20kettle.md]]. Leading with the kettle and a person is what sold it.
- Fee agreed before writing: $900 for 3,000 words, payment on publication.
