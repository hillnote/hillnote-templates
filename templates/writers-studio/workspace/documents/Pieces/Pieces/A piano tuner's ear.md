**One line:** Agnes Moll has tuned the same concert hall piano for twenty-six years. What does it mean to listen for a living?

**Structure:** Braided. Three strands: one tuning session, the physics of what she listens for, and my own childhood piano lessons.

## Outline

**Strand A: the hall, one morning**

1. Agnes arrives at seven, before the cleaners. The hall is cold and the piano is colder.
2. The tuning fork, the felt mutes, the lever. One unison at a time.
3. She stops and listens to the room, not the piano.

**Strand B: what she listens for**

1. Two strings that are almost in tune "beat" against each other: a slow wobble in the sound. Tuning is listening for the beats to slow down and stop.
2. Equal temperament: every interval except the octave is tuned very slightly wrong, so that every key can be played. A deliberate, shared compromise.

**Strand C: me**

1. Seven years of lessons, and I never once noticed the piano was out of tune.
2. What I can hear now, and what I still can't.

**The braid:** equal temperament as a way of thinking about repair. Perfect isn't the goal; usable everywhere is.
