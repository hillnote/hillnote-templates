## Draft, first half

The queue starts at quarter to ten, fifteen minutes before the doors open. Nobody seems to mind. A woman has a toaster in a carrier bag. A teenager has a games controller with a sticky button and the expression of someone who has been sent by his mother. Near the front, a man holds a brass lamp upright in both arms, the flex wound neatly around its base, the way you might hold a baby you have been handed at a christening.

Inside, the church hall has been rearranged into stations: electrical by the stage, textiles by the kitchen hatch, bicycles out on the forecourt. Each station has a volunteer and a handwritten sign. The electrical sign says, in capitals, WE WILL TRY.
