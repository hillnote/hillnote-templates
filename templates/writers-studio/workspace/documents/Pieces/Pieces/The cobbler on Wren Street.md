**One line:** a cobbler who has resoled the same pair of boots for eleven years, and what it means to make something last longer than the person who made it.

**Structure:** Book chapter: scene, backstory, craft, widening, return, question. See the [[Structure cheat-sheets|doc:documents/Craft/Structure%20cheat-sheets.md]].

## Outline

1. **Scene.** Teodor at the bench, holding a boot up to the window. The split welt. "You don't fix a shoe."
2. **Backstory.** Apprenticed to his uncle at fifteen. The shop has had three names and one smell.
3. **The craft.** Lasts, welts, the difference between gluing and stitching. Why a stitched welt can be resoled again and again and a glued sole can't.
4. **The customers.** The boots that come back every two winters. The man who brings his late wife's shoes to be kept, not worn.
5. **The widening.** What we lose when an object outlives our ability to look after it.
6. **Return.** The boot goes back on the shelf with ticket 4471.
7. **The question.** What do you own that someone else could keep alive?

## Draft excerpt

Teodor holds the boot up to the window like a jeweller with a stone. The welt has split along the inside edge, the way it does when someone has walked on the outsides of their feet for twenty years. He runs a thumbnail along the seam and says nothing for long enough that I wonder whether I should leave.

"You don't fix a shoe," he says eventually. "You fix the way someone walks."

He puts the boot down, writes a ticket, and tears it in half. The half with the number goes to me. The other half goes into the heel, where it will stay until the boot is finished, and where, he tells me later, he has found tickets from his uncle's time, forty years old, still legible.
