Two years in a cabin by a pond, and a long argument about what we actually need.

**Read for:** the first chapter, "Economy", which is the book's opening argument and the part that matters for repair.

**Notes**

- Thoreau measures cost in time, not money: the price of a thing is the part of your life you trade for it.
- He builds his own house and lists every expense to the half-cent. The list is funnier than he meant it to be.
- A useful tension: he is against owning more than you need, but never against making things well.

**Quote**

> "The cost of a thing is the amount of what I will call life which is required to be exchanged for it, immediately or in the long run."
