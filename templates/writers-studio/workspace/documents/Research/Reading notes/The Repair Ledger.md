A history of one city's small repair shops, told through the account books they left behind: what got fixed, what it cost, and when the entries stop.

**Notes so far**

- The shoe repair chapter tracks one shop across thirty years. Jobs per week fall by two-thirds; the price of each job triples. Fewer customers, each one more committed.
- The ledgers record names against repeat items ("Mrs P., brown brogues, again"). Repair was a relationship as much as a service.
- Kell's method could work for the book: ask Teodor whether his uncle kept account books.
