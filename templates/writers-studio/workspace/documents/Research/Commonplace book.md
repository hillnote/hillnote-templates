# Commonplace book

A commonplace book is the oldest writer's tool there is: a notebook of things worth keeping. Quotes, overheard lines, facts, your own half-sentences. Every entry gets a source, a topic and, when it has one, the piece it's for.

Click a topic such as #craft or #care to see every page that carries it, including the pieces and the reading notes.

**How to add an entry**

1. The quote or observation, exactly as you found it.
2. Who said it, and where. If it's yours, say so.
3. One or two topics.
4. The piece it's for, if you know.

Agnes Moll, piano tuner, on why she tunes on the morning of a concert and never the day before. #attention · for [[A piano tuner's ear|doc:documents/Pieces/Pieces/A%20piano%20tuner%27s%20ear.md]]

## Time and money

Henry David Thoreau, *Walden*, in the chapter "Economy". Public domain. #money #time · see [[Walden|doc:documents/Research/Reading%20notes/Walden.md]]

> "There is no wealth but life."

John Ruskin, *Unto This Last*. Public domain. #money #care · see [[Unto This Last|doc:documents/Research/Reading%20notes/Unto%20This%20Last.md]]

> "A guarantee is a promise about the next year. A repair is a promise about the next ten."

My own line, cut from the Harbor Review essay. Keep it for the book. #repair #time
