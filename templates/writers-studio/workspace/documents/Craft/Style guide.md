# Style guide

The rules ${pen_name} writes by. A style guide exists to end arguments quickly, mostly with yourself. When a rule gets in the way of a good sentence, the sentence wins and the rule gets a footnote.

:::toc "On this page"
:::

## Voice

- **Plain over clever.** If a sentence needs reading twice, rewrite it once.
- **Specific over general.** "A size-nine brogue with a split welt", not "an old shoe".
- **Warm, not soft.** Say the hard thing kindly; don't skip it.
- **First person, sparingly.** "I" is for scenes and admissions, not for opinions dressed up as findings.

## Sentences

- Vary the length. Three long sentences in a row need a short one after them.
- Keep most sentences under 25 words. One sentence a paragraph can run long if it earns it.
- Put the important word at the end of the sentence, where the stress falls.
- [The read-aloud test]{#read-aloud}: if you run out of breath, the sentence is too long. If you stumble, the rhythm is wrong. The prose check in the [[Editing checklist|doc:documents/Craft/Editing%20checklist.md]] can read a passage back to you.

## Paragraphs

- One idea per paragraph. The newsletter gets shorter paragraphs than the book: three or four sentences, then air.
- Open a paragraph with its point. Save the point for the last line only when the paragraph is a turn.

## Words to cut

| Cut | Because |
|---|---|
| very, really, quite | They weaken the word they lean on. Find a stronger word |
| just, actually, simply | Filler. Read the sentence without them |
| in order to | "to" does the job |
| it is important to note that | If it's important, say it |
| sort of, kind of | Commit, or explain the doubt |
| utilise, leverage | use |

## Punctuation and numbers

**Commas**

- Serial comma: yes. "Needles, thread, and patience."
- No comma splices. Use two sentences, a semicolon, or a dash.

**Dashes**

- Em dash without spaces for a break in thought. Two per page at most.
- En dash for ranges in tables; "to" in running text.

**Numbers**

- Spell out one to nine; use figures from 10. Always figures for measurements and money.
- Thousands take a comma: 2,100 readers.

## Names and titles

- Book titles in italics: *${book_title}*. The newsletter's name in bold the first time it appears in an issue: **${newsletter_name}**.
- Real people in the book are named only with their permission; otherwise a first name and one changed detail.
- Places by street, not by postcode.

## Quotes and sources

- Quote exactly or paraphrase honestly. Never tidy a quote into a better sentence.
- Every quote goes into the [[Commonplace book|doc:documents/Research/Commonplace%20book.md]], with its source, on the day you collect it.
- Every fact and figure links to a row in **Reading notes**.

## Newsletter house style

- Subject lines under 50 characters, no clickbait, no capitals for emphasis. See the [[headline patterns|doc:documents/Craft/Structure%20cheat-sheets.md#headlines]].
- One link per idea, and never "click here".
- Every issue signs off the same way: "Mend something this week", then ${pen_name}.
