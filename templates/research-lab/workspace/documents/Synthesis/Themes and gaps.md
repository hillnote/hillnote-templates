## Themes

### Restoration

Holding a plot improves gardeners' own mental wellbeing, and the best-designed study in the set shows it. Benefits are largest for people living alone. **Confidence: moderate**, because it rests on one cohort study. #restoration

Evidence: [[Taking up a plot improves mental wellbeing for gardeners|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Taking%20up%20a%20plot%20improves%20mental%20wellbeing%20for%20gardeners.md]]

### Connection

Gardens add social ties, but mostly among people who already knew each other. Benefits reach non-gardeners mainly through everyday contact at the boundary: the fence, the gate, the produce left on the wall. **Confidence: moderate.** #social-cohesion

Evidence: [[Benefits reach non-gardeners through everyday contact at the fence|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Benefits%20reach%20non-gardeners%20through%20everyday%20contact%20at%20the%20fence.md]], [[Gardens mostly deepen ties among people who already knew each other|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Gardens%20mostly%20deepen%20ties%20among%20people%20who%20already%20knew%20each%20other.md]], [[Gardens work as third places where people linger without spending money|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Gardens%20work%20as%20third%20places%20where%20people%20linger%20without%20spending%20money.md]]

### Health and diet

Gardeners eat a little more fruit and veg and move a little more. Effects are small and self-selected. **Confidence: low.** #food-and-health

Evidence: [[Gardeners eat more fruit and vegetables, but the effect is small|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Gardeners%20eat%20more%20fruit%20and%20vegetables%2C%20but%20the%20effect%20is%20small.md]]

### Access and exclusion

Who benefits depends on who gets in. Waiting lists, residency rules and long tenures favour settled owners. Open governance and reserved plots seem to help. **Confidence: moderate** for the problem, **low** for the fixes. #access-and-equity

Evidence: [[Waiting lists and tenure rules shut out newer and renting residents|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Waiting%20lists%20and%20tenure%20rules%20shut%20out%20newer%20and%20renting%20residents.md]], [[Shared governance widens who takes part|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Shared%20governance%20widens%20who%20takes%20part.md]]

### Change

Over years, gardens may help raise rents and change who lives nearby, which would hand the benefits to newcomers. **Confidence: low**, from case studies only. #green-gentrification

Evidence: [[New gardens can speed up green gentrification|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/New%20gardens%20can%20speed%20up%20green%20gentrification.md]]

## Tensions

- **Connection against exclusion.** The same mechanism, a tight group of regulars, makes a garden feel like a community to insiders and a club to everyone else. [[P04|doc:documents/Findings/Interview%20notes%20-%20P04.md]] lives both sides of it.
- **Short term against long term.** Most studies measure the first one to three years. Gentrification works over five to ten, so the evidence is weighted towards the good news.

## Gaps

| Gap | Why it matters | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| No study follows non-gardeners over time | H1 is about them, and the evidence is all snapshots | Propose the phase 2 survey with a baseline before the new gardens open |
| Renters are almost absent | They are the group most likely to be excluded | Recruit P09 to P12 among renters; search housing association reports |
| Rents near new gardens are unmeasured | The Change theme rests on case studies | New search: Garden AND (rent OR "house price*"); ask the council for rent data |
| Produce sharing is mentioned but never measured | It is a direct spillover to non-gardeners | Add a probe to the interview guide; track the Food sharing code |
| The third-place claim is second-hand | It could reach the draft unverified | Read Gardens as third places before citing it |
