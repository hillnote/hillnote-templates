## Summary

A policy report reviewing the allocation rules of 18 municipal garden schemes, with interviews with 30 people who had applied for a plot. The median wait was three and a half years. Schemes that asked for proof of two or more years' residence, or that let plot holders pass plots to relatives, had the fewest renters and recent arrivals among their members.

## Key claims

1. Allocation rules, not lack of interest, explain who holds plots. Logged as [[Waiting lists and tenure rules shut out newer and renting residents|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Waiting%20lists%20and%20tenure%20rules%20shut%20out%20newer%20and%20renting%20residents.md]].
2. Plots are rarely given up: the median tenure was eleven years.
3. Schemes that reserved a share of plots for new applicants had more varied membership.

## Method

Document review of published allocation rules, plus semi-structured interviews with applicants recruited through waiting lists. Grey literature, so not peer reviewed, and the interviewees were people who had waited, not people who never applied.

## Quotes

> "The waiting list is the policy. Everything else is decoration." (p. 14)

## My thoughts

Not peer reviewed, but the documentary evidence is hard to argue with, and it matches what P02 and P05 told me almost word for word. This is the backbone of RQ3. It also suggests a concrete recommendation for the committee: reserve plots for new applicants. #access-and-equity
