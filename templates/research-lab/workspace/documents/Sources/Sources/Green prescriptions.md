## Summary

A three-year cohort of 1,240 adults. New allotment holders were compared with people still on the waiting list for the same sites, matched on age, sex and area deprivation. After twelve months the new gardeners scored about 0.3 standard deviations higher on a wellbeing scale, and the gap held at three years. They also reported about 1.5 more hours of physical activity a week.

## Key claims

1. Taking up a plot improves mental wellbeing, against a waiting-list comparison group. Logged as [[Taking up a plot improves mental wellbeing for gardeners|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Taking%20up%20a%20plot%20improves%20mental%20wellbeing%20for%20gardeners.md]].
2. The effect appears within a year and lasts at least three.
3. Physical activity rises too, though it was self-reported.

## Method

Prospective cohort with waiting-list controls. That is a clever design: both groups wanted a plot, so motivation is roughly balanced. Losses to follow-up were 22% among gardeners and 31% among controls, which may flatter the gardeners.

## Quotes

> "Gaining a plot, rather than wanting one, accounted for the difference." (p. 309)

> "Effects were largest among participants living alone." (p. 311)

## My thoughts
