## Summary

Notes so far. A chapter arguing that new gardens in lower-income districts can become part of how those districts are marketed, and so contribute to rising rents and the displacement of long-term residents. Two districts serve as case studies, with estate-agent listings and planning documents as evidence.

## Key claims

1. New gardens can speed up green gentrification. Logged as [[New gardens can speed up green gentrification|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/New%20gardens%20can%20speed%20up%20green%20gentrification.md]].
2. Long-term residents may lose the benefits of a garden by being priced out of the area.

## Method

Two qualitative case studies, built from documents and listings. There is no rent data over time, so the causal story is plausible but unproven.

## Quotes

> "The garden appears in the brochure before it appears in the rent." (p. 97)

## My thoughts

The strongest challenge to H1 so far. It works on a longer timescale than any of the other studies. P04's "it's on the estate agent's leaflet now" fits uncomfortably well.
