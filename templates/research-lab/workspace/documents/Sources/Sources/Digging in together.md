## Summary

A survey of 610 residents living near nine community gardens in three mid-sized cities, plus 24 follow-up interviews. Gardeners named more people in the neighbourhood they could ask for help than non-gardeners did. Non-gardeners living close to a garden reported slightly more local contacts than those further away, but the difference was small.

## Key claims

1. Gardeners name more local contacts than non-gardeners: a mean of 7.2 against 4.9. Logged as [[Gardens mostly deepen ties among people who already knew each other|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Gardens%20mostly%20deepen%20ties%20among%20people%20who%20already%20knew%20each%20other.md]].
2. Most new ties formed between people who already knew each other by sight. The garden thickens existing ties more than it creates new ones. Also in [[Gardens mostly deepen ties among people who already knew each other|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Gardens%20mostly%20deepen%20ties%20among%20people%20who%20already%20knew%20each%20other.md]].
3. Non-gardeners within 200 metres report a little more trust than those further away (d = 0.18). Weak support for [[Benefits reach non-gardeners through everyday contact at the fence|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Benefits%20reach%20non-gardeners%20through%20everyday%20contact%20at%20the%20fence.md]].

## Method

Cross-sectional survey, with address-based sampling within 600 metres of each garden; 41% response rate. Interviews were with a purposive sub-sample of survey respondents. There is no baseline, so people who were already sociable may simply be more likely to garden.

## Quotes

> "The garden rarely introduces strangers; it gives acquaintances a reason to keep talking." (p. 124)

> "Proximity mattered less than participation." (p. 127)
