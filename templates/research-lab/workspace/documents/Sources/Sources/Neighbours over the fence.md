## Summary

Notes so far, up to section 4. A survey of 1,020 residents who don't garden, living at different distances from 12 community gardens. Those within 150 metres reported more trust in neighbours and a stronger sense of belonging. The effect was concentrated among people who said they talked to gardeners at least once a month.

## Key claims

1. Non-gardeners near a garden report more trust and belonging, mostly through regular contact with gardeners. Logged as [[Benefits reach non-gardeners through everyday contact at the fence|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Benefits%20reach%20non-gardeners%20through%20everyday%20contact%20at%20the%20fence.md]].
2. Renters were less likely than owners to report any contact with the garden.

## Method

Cross-sectional household survey with distance bands. They adjust for income and length of residence. Still to check: how they dealt with gardens being sited in already-cohesive areas.

## Quotes

> "The fence, not the plot, is where the neighbourhood meets the garden." (p. 31)

## My thoughts

The only study that asks non-gardeners directly, so it matters more than its design alone suggests. Its "contact at the fence" mechanism matches P04 almost exactly ([[Interview notes - P04|doc:documents/Findings/Interview%20notes%20-%20P04.md]]). Point 2 links the connection theme to the access theme: the people left out of plots are also less likely to benefit from the fence.
