## Summary

A systematic review of 14 studies on diet among community gardeners. Gardeners ate about 0.6 more portions of fruit and vegetables a day than comparison groups. The studies varied widely, and most were cross-sectional.

## Key claims

1. Gardeners eat somewhat more fruit and vegetables. Logged as [[Gardeners eat more fruit and vegetables, but the effect is small|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Gardeners%20eat%20more%20fruit%20and%20vegetables%2C%20but%20the%20effect%20is%20small.md]].
2. The effect is smaller in better-designed studies.
3. Sharing produce with neighbours was mentioned in five studies but never measured.

## Method

A registered protocol, searches of four databases and a risk-of-bias assessment. Nine of the 14 studies were rated at high risk of bias, mostly because of self-selection. No meta-analysis, because the outcomes were measured too differently.

## Quotes

> "The best-controlled studies report the smallest effects." (p. 58)

## My thoughts

Useful mainly as a brake: the diet benefit is real but small, and the committee should not be promised healthier eating across the neighbourhood. Point 3 is a gap worth noting. Produce sharing is exactly the kind of spillover H1 is about, and nobody has measured it. #food-and-health
