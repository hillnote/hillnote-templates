# Research plan

Twelve weeks, two strands. A structured literature review answers RQ1 and part of RQ2. Semi-structured interviews at three gardens answer the rest of RQ2 and most of RQ3, because the published research says little about who gets left out. Both strands feed the same Claims and evidence database, so they are synthesised together rather than reported side by side.

## Literature review method

1. **Search.** Three bibliographic databases plus Google Scholar and grey literature, using the concept blocks in the [[Search log|doc:documents/Question/Search%20log.md]]. Aim for about ${target_sources} included sources.
2. **Screen.** Title and abstract against the criteria in [[Question and scope|doc:documents/Question/Question%20and%20scope.md]], then full text. A second reader screens a 20% sample, and disagreements are talked through and noted in the log.
3. **Appraise.** Every included source gets a short critical appraisal in its row: is the design right for the question, how big and how biased is the sample, and do the conclusions follow from the data? `/critique_source` drafts one to start from.
4. **Extract.** Each finding that might appear in the review becomes a row in Claims and evidence, with its stance on H1, the strength of the evidence and the page number.
5. **Synthesise.** Build the synthesis matrix (sources against themes), write a narrative synthesis by theme, and grade confidence in each theme as high, moderate or low.

## Interview method

- **Sampling:** purposive. Four people at each of Hollins Road Community Garden, Mill Lane Allotments and St Brigid's Garden: two plot holders and two neighbours who don't garden, varying age, tenure and how long they have lived in the area.
- **Guide:** semi-structured, about 45 minutes, see the [[Interview guide|doc:documents/Question/Interview%20guide.md]].
- **Analysis:** reflexive thematic analysis. Familiarise, code, generate themes, review them, define them, write up. Codes live in the **Codebook** sheet with a definition and inclusion rules, so a second coder can apply them consistently.
- **Saturation check:** after interview 10, list any code that appeared for the first time in the last three interviews. If there are none, stop at 12.

## Risks

| Risk | Plan |
|---|---|
| Recruiting non-gardeners is slow | Leaflets through doors near each garden, plus the residents' associations |
| The literature is mostly about gardeners, not neighbours | Treat it as a finding; the interviews fill the gap |
| Small sample, three gardens | Be explicit that the interviews explain mechanisms, not prevalence |
