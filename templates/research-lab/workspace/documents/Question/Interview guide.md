# Interview guide

Semi-structured, about 45 minutes. The questions are a map, not a script: follow the participant, and use the probes when an answer stays general. Every question traces back to a research question in [[Question and scope|doc:documents/Question/Question%20and%20scope.md]].

## Before the interview

- [ ] Consent form signed and a copy left with the participant
- [ ] Recorder tested; second recorder on the table as a backup
- [ ] Participant code noted (P01 to P12), and no names in the notes
- [ ] Ten minutes after the interview kept free for the memo

## Opening (5 minutes)

> Thanks for meeting me. I'm studying how community gardens affect the neighbourhoods around them. There are no right answers: I'm interested in your experience, including anything negative. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Is it still all right to record?

1. How long have you lived around here? What's the neighbourhood like?

## Using the garden (RQ1, 10 minutes)

2. Tell me about the last time you were in or near the garden.
    - *Probe:* What were you doing? Who else was there?
3. What, if anything, does the garden do for you?
    - *Probe:* How do you feel when you leave? Has it changed what you eat or how active you are?

## The garden and the neighbourhood (RQ2, 15 minutes)

4. Has the garden changed how you know or relate to your neighbours?
    - *Probe:* Can you give me an example? Was that someone you knew already?
5. How do people who don't garden see it?
    - *Probe:* Do they use it? Do they feel welcome?
6. Does the garden change how the street feels, during the day or at night?

## Access (RQ3, 10 minutes)

7. How does someone get a plot, or get involved?
    - *Probe:* How long did that take you? Who decides?
8. Is there anyone who you think would like to be involved but isn't?
    - *Probe:* What stops them?
9. If the council built a new garden near here, what should it do differently?

## Closing (5 minutes)

10. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
11. Would you like to see the quotes I use before the report goes out?

## Good habits

- **Ask for stories, not opinions.** "Tell me about the last time…" gets you evidence. "Do you think gardens are good?" gets you politeness.
- **Silence is a probe.** Count to five before moving on.
- **Don't lead.** Not "Did it make you feel less lonely?" but "How did you feel afterwards?"
- **Write the memo straight away.** What surprised you, what you'd ask next time, and how your own view might have shaped the conversation.

Write each interview up with the [[Interview notes|doc:documents/Templates/Interview%20notes.md]] template, as in [[Interview notes - P04|doc:documents/Findings/Interview%20notes%20-%20P04.md]].
