# Interview notes: P04

> ==Sample interview, fictional participant.== Written to show what a coded interview write-up looks like.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Participant** | P04, neighbour who doesn't garden |
| **Site** | Hollins Road Community Garden |
| **Lives** | Opposite the garden, renting, nine years in the area |
| **Interview** | 48 minutes, at a café nearby, recorded with consent |
| **Codes applied** | Fence talk, Watching over, Food sharing, Waiting list, Rents going up |

## Summary

P04 has never held a plot and doesn't want one, yet describes the garden as "the reason I know anyone on this street". Most of their contact happens across the railings: produce on the wall, conversations about what is growing, people stopping to talk on the way past. They feel safer walking home past it. They also put their name on the waiting list once and gave up, and they worry that the garden makes the street more attractive to people who can pay more rent.

## Key moments

- **12:40** The fence as a meeting place: "I don't garden, but I know everyone's tomatoes by now." Coded *Fence talk*. Supports [[Benefits reach non-gardeners through everyday contact at the fence|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Benefits%20reach%20non-gardeners%20through%20everyday%20contact%20at%20the%20fence.md]].
- **18:15** Produce left on the wall with a sign saying "help yourself". Coded *Food sharing*.
- **24:30** Walking home past the garden at night: "Someone's always there till dark." Coded *Watching over*.
- **31:50** The waiting list: "Four years on the list. I've stopped asking." Coded *Waiting list*. Supports [[Waiting lists and tenure rules shut out newer and renting residents|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/Waiting%20lists%20and%20tenure%20rules%20shut%20out%20newer%20and%20renting%20residents.md]].
- **40:10** "It's lovely. It's also on the estate agent's leaflet now." Coded *Rents going up*. Relates to [[New gardens can speed up green gentrification|doc:documents/Findings/Claims%20and%20evidence/New%20gardens%20can%20speed%20up%20green%20gentrification.md]].

## Memo

The strongest account so far of benefits reaching a non-gardener, and it runs almost entirely through the boundary of the garden, not through membership. It is also the clearest case of one person experiencing both sides of H1: connected by the garden, shut out of it, and uneasy about what it signals.

I may have led at 31:50 by asking "was it hard to get a plot?" instead of "how does someone get a plot?". Check the transcript, and use the neutral wording from the interview guide next time.
