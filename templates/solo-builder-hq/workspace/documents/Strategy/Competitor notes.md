## Spreadsheet and a doc template

What most people really use: three of six interviewees. Free, flexible, and nothing at all happens after "send".

**Strength:** no learning curve and total control over the layout.

**Gap:** no reminders, no payment link, and "what's owed" means opening two files.

**Our angle:** make switching take ten minutes. Recreate their layout and bring their client list across for them.

## Countwell

The full accounting suite. [[Marco|doc:documents/Strategy/Customer%20interviews/Marco%20-%20freelance%20developer.md]] uses it: "It does everything, which is the problem."

**Strength:** tax reports, bank sync, an accountant who already knows it.

**Gap:** price, setup time, and reminders that are a single generic email most people switch off.

**Our angle:** don't compete on accounting. Do one job better, and export cleanly to whatever the accountant uses.

## Slipsend

A free invoice maker. [[Tom|doc:documents/Strategy/Customer%20interviews/Tom%20-%20wedding%20photographer.md]] uses it for one-off jobs.

**Strength:** fast and free.

**Gap:** ads on the invoice, no reminders, no record of what's been paid.

**Our angle:** the first invoice is just as fast, and everything after it is the part that's missing.

## Bank invoicing

Built into some business bank accounts.

**Strength:** free, and payments reconcile on their own.

**Gap:** bank transfer only, clunky templates, no reminders.

**Our angle:** card payments and follow-ups. A bank could add both, so watch it.

## Positioning statement

> For solo freelancers who hate chasing money, ${product_name} is the invoicing app that follows up for you. Unlike accounting suites, it does one job well and takes a minute to learn.

## What would worry us

- Countwell ships tone-aware reminders and a cheap plan for solo users.
- Banks add card payment links to their free invoicing.
- A payments provider bundles reminders into its own invoices for free.

Check this list once a month. If one of them happens, run `/pressure_test` with the news.
